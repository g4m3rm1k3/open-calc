## Concept Unit: The Most Common System Killers

### The Problem
Even good systems eventually face predictable threats: perfectionism, life disruptions, motivation crashes, and scope creep. We need to name them in advance so we can defend against them.

### Introduce the concept in isolation
**Throwaway lab** (list these on paper):

- Perfectionism (“If I can’t do it perfectly, I won’t do it at all”)
- All-or-nothing thinking (“I missed one day → the whole system is ruined”)
- Overloading the system too quickly
- No maintenance routine

Reflect for a moment: Which of these has killed your past efforts?

**What this proves**: Naming the killers in advance dramatically reduces their power.

### Mechanical walkthrough

### Systems Thinking Lens
This is **pre-mortem analysis** — imagining how the system could fail and building defenses before it happens. A core practice in resilient systems.

### Engineering / Design Lens
Anticipating failure modes is standard engineering practice (FMEA — Failure Modes and Effects Analysis).  
**Alternative not chosen**: Hoping your system will just keep working.

## Concept Unit: Motivation Management and System Maintenance

### The Problem
Motivation is unreliable. We need strategies that work when motivation is low, and a light maintenance habit to keep systems healthy.

### The New Code — type it yourself
Add to `lesson7.md`:

```markdown
## Motivation Management Rules
(Examples: “Do the minimum viable version”, “Never miss twice”, “Focus on identity: I am the kind of person who...”)

## My System Maintenance Habit
(When and how will I review all my systems? e.g. every Sunday for 15 minutes)
```

### The Updated Project
Your workbook now includes motivation rules and a maintenance habit.

### Mechanical walkthrough
1. **Motivation rules** — Pre-commit to behaviors that work even on bad days.
2. **Maintenance habit** — A recurring time to review and tweak all your systems.

### Systems Thinking Lens
Motivation is a lagging indicator, not a driver. Identity-based habits and “never miss twice” rules create momentum that survives low-motivation periods.

### Engineering / Design Lens
Maintenance is scheduled preventive care (like regular car servicing).  
**Alternative not chosen**: Running systems until they break, then fixing them.  
**Tradeoff**: Regular light maintenance takes discipline, but prevents total system collapse and major recovery costs.

### Run it
Choose 1–2 motivation rules and schedule your first maintenance session.

### Connecting sentence
We now have defenses against failure and tools for long-term maintenance.
