## Concept Unit: Habit Stacking

### The Problem
Even with a good system, starting the action can feel hard. “I’ll do it later” is the enemy. We need a reliable way to trigger the behavior automatically.

### Introduce the concept in isolation
**Throwaway lab** (write this):

**Simple stack**:  
After I make my morning coffee → I will read for 10 minutes.

**Stronger stack**:  
After I brush my teeth (already automatic) → I will put on workout clothes → I will do 5 push-ups.

Try one stack for 2 days.

**What this proves**: Linking a new behavior to an existing habit (anchoring) makes it much more likely to happen.

**Discard the throwaway example.**

### Project Change
- **Files affected**: Continue in `systems-mastery/lesson4.md`

### The New Code — type it yourself
Add to your workbook:

```markdown
# Systems Mastery - Lesson 4

## My Current System
(From previous lessons)

## Habit Stack Design
After [existing habit] → I will [new action]

## My Refined Stack
(Write your version here)
```

### The Updated Project
Your workbook now has a habit stacking template.

### Mechanical walkthrough
1. **Existing habit** — Choose something you already do reliably every day (brushing teeth, making coffee, opening laptop, etc.).
2. **New action** — Attach your desired behavior immediately after the existing habit.
3. **Refined Stack** — Write the full chain clearly.

### Systems Thinking Lens
This creates a **cue-response loop**. The existing habit becomes the trigger (cue) for the new behavior. Strong cues reduce the mental effort needed to start.

### Engineering / Design Lens
Habit stacking is friction reduction engineering.  
**Alternative not chosen**: Relying on willpower or motivation (“I’ll just do it when I feel like it”).  
**Tradeoff**: Stacking requires thought about your current routines, but it makes execution far more automatic and reliable.

### Run it
Create and start using at least one habit stack for your system today.

## Concept Unit: Environment Design

### The Problem
Your physical and digital environment often works against your system (phone notifications, junk food in sight, messy desk, etc.). We can redesign small parts of the environment to support the system instead.

### The New Code — type it yourself
Add to `lesson4.md`:

```markdown
## Environment Design Changes
- Make the desired action easier / more visible:
- Make the bad action harder / less visible:
```

### The Updated Project
Your workbook now includes environment adjustments.

### Mechanical walkthrough
1. **Make desired action easier** — Reduce friction (e.g., put workout clothes out the night before, keep book on desk).
2. **Make bad action harder** — Increase friction (e.g., put phone in another room, hide snacks).

### Systems Thinking Lens
This is **leveraging the environment** as part of the system. Your surroundings become part of the feedback loop instead of an obstacle.

### Engineering / Design Lens
Environment design is constraint-based engineering. You shape the system by changing the context rather than fighting it.  
**Tradeoff**: It takes some upfront effort to change your environment, but it reduces daily decision fatigue.

### Run it
Make at least one small environment change today that supports your system.

### Connecting sentence
We now have tools to make systems easier to run (habit stacking + environment design).
