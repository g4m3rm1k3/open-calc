**Transferable problems**: How to close the loop between intention and reality, and how to continuously improve any system instead of abandoning it.

## Concept Unit: Why Tracking Matters

### The Problem
Without visibility, you don’t know if your system is working. Many people run systems blindly for weeks, then get discouraged because they have no data.

### Introduce the concept in isolation
**Throwaway lab** (do this for 2 days):

Track one simple daily action with just a checkmark (✓) in a note or calendar.

**What this proves**: The simple act of recording whether you did the action creates awareness and usually improves consistency (this is called the Hawthorne effect + basic feedback).

**Discard the throwaway example.**

### Project Change
- Continue in `systems-mastery/lesson5.md`

### The New Code — type it yourself
Add to your workbook:

```markdown
# Systems Mastery - Lesson 5

## My Tracking Method
(How will I record completion? e.g. calendar, notebook, app, simple list)

## My Weekly Review Questions
1. How many times did I execute the system this week?
2. What went well?
3. What was difficult?
4. What one small change could improve it?
```

### The Updated Project
Your workbook now has tracking + review templates.

### Mechanical walkthrough
1. **Tracking Method** — Choose the simplest possible way to record success/failure (the easier it is, the more likely you’ll do it).
2. **Weekly Review** — A regular time to look at your data and ask the four questions.

### Systems Thinking Lens
This creates a **closed feedback loop**: Action → Observation (tracking) → Reflection (review) → Adjustment. Without the loop, your system cannot improve itself.

### Engineering / Design Lens
Tracking + review is the equivalent of quality control or debugging in engineering.  
**Alternative not chosen**: “Just do your best and hope.”  
**Tradeoff**: Adding tracking and review takes a small amount of extra time each week, but it prevents months of wasted effort on broken systems.

### Run it
Choose a tracking method and do your first weekly review (even if it’s only a few days of data).

## Concept Unit: Iteration — Improving the System

### The Problem
Your first version of a system is almost never optimal. You must learn how to make small, smart improvements instead of quitting or overhauling everything.

### The New Code — type it yourself
Add to `lesson5.md`:

```markdown
## Iteration Plan
Based on my review, one small change I will make next week:
```

### The Updated Project
Your workbook now includes an iteration step.

### Mechanical walkthrough
1. Look at tracking data.
2. Answer the review questions honestly.
3. Pick **one small change** (not a complete overhaul).
4. Update your system and test it.

### Systems Thinking Lens
This is **iterative improvement** — the core of any evolving system. Small, frequent adjustments beat big, infrequent ones.

### Engineering / Design Lens
Iteration is the practical application of the scientific method to personal life: hypothesize (make a change), test (run it), measure (track), learn (review).  
**Tradeoff**: It requires humility (admitting the system isn’t perfect yet) and consistency, but it turns average systems into excellent ones over time.

### Run it
Make one small improvement to your current system based on your review.

### Connecting sentence
We now have the full basic cycle: Design → Execute → Track → Review → Iterate.
