## Concept Unit: Managing Multiple Goals Without Overload

### The Problem
Once you have systems working in one area (e.g. health), you want to add others (learning, career, relationships). Doing too many at once usually causes everything to collapse.

### Introduce the concept in isolation
**Throwaway lab** (write these):

**Bad approach**: Start 5 new systems at the same time.  
**Better approach**: Run 1–2 core systems at full strength, and 1–2 “maintenance mode” systems (very low effort).

Try the better approach mentally for one week.

**What this proves**: Focusing energy on a few systems while lightly maintaining others leads to better overall results.

**Discard the throwaway example.**

### Project Change
- Continue in `systems-mastery/lesson8.md`

### The New Code — type it yourself
Add to your workbook:

```markdown
# Systems Mastery - Lesson 8

## My Current Active Systems
(List them)

## Prioritization
- Core Systems (full focus, 1-2 max):
- Maintenance Mode (light effort):
- Future Systems (queued):
```

### The Updated Project
Your workbook now has a prioritization framework for multiple goals.

### Mechanical walkthrough
1. **List current systems** — Get everything visible.
2. **Core Systems** — Choose 1–2 that get your best energy right now.
3. **Maintenance Mode** — Systems you want to keep alive but with minimal effort.
4. **Queued** — Goals you will activate later.

### Systems Thinking Lens
This is **capacity management** and **prioritization**. Every system consumes attention and energy — you must manage total load like a machine manages power.

### Engineering / Design Lens
This follows resource allocation principles.  
**Alternative not chosen**: Treating every goal as equally important and trying to do everything at full intensity.  
**Tradeoff**: Saying “not now” to some goals feels restrictive, but it prevents total burnout and produces better results in the areas that matter most.

## Concept Unit: Life Area Integration and Long-Term Evolution

### The Problem
Life has multiple domains (health, relationships, career, learning, finances, fun, contribution). Systems in one area affect others. We also change over time, so our systems must evolve.

### The New Code — type it yourself
Add to `lesson8.md`:

```markdown
## Life Area Balance Check
(Health, Relationships, Career/Learning, Finances, Fun/Recovery)
- Which areas are strong?
- Which areas are neglected?

## Long-Term Evolution Plan
(Every 3 months I will...)
```

### The Updated Project
Your workbook now includes balance checking and evolution planning.

### Mechanical walkthrough
1. **Life Area Balance Check** — Review how your systems are distributed.
2. **Long-Term Evolution Plan** — Schedule periodic reviews (e.g. every 3 months) to adapt your systems as your life changes.

### Systems Thinking Lens
This is **holistic system design** — understanding that all areas of life are interconnected. Neglecting one area eventually drags down the others.

### Engineering / Design Lens
Long-term evolution is **versioning and refactoring**. Your life systems are software — they need periodic updates.  
**Alternative not chosen**: Setting systems once and never changing them.  
**Tradeoff**: Regular reviews take time, but they keep your systems relevant and prevent them from becoming obsolete or harmful as you grow.

### Run it
Do a quick life area balance check and schedule your first 3-month evolution review.

You now have tools to manage multiple goals, balance life areas, and evolve your systems over years.
